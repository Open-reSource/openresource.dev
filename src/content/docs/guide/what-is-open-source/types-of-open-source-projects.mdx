<p class="lead">Open source started as a way to ship software and became a way to ship anything with a source: a circuit board, a map, an encyclopedia, a standard. The artifact changes, the rules don't: a license that lets anyone use, change and share it, a place to contribute, and people who maintain it. Here is the map, with a real project in each box.</p>

<InShort>

- Software is most of open source, from the kernel on the world's supercomputers to the framework in your dependencies.
- Beyond software, the source is a design (Arduino), a dataset (OpenStreetMap), a text (Wikipedia), a paper (arXiv) or a standard (HTML).
- The rules are the same everywhere, so the rest of this guide talks about software and the advice translates.
- A NonCommercial license makes content free to read, not open: MIT OpenCourseWare and Khan Academy are in that case.

</InShort>

The boxes differ in what they ship, not in how they work. **Every one of them needs a license that grants the freedoms, a way to contribute and someone who maintains it.** That's why the rest of this guide talks about software: replace "pull request" with "edit" and "release" with "dataset dump", and the advice holds.

## Zoom on Open-Source Software

### Operating systems

[Linux](https://www.kernel.org/) runs every one of the 500 systems on the [June 2026 TOP500 list](https://www.top500.org/statistics/details/osfam/1/) of the fastest supercomputers. Distributions package it with everything else: [Debian](https://www.debian.org/intro/about) has over 70,000 packages and almost a thousand active developers, as of September 2026.

### Programming languages

The languages most code is written in are open source themselves, each under its own license: [Rust](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust) under MIT and Apache 2.0, [Go](https://github.com/golang/go) under BSD-3-Clause, [TypeScript](https://github.com/microsoft/TypeScript) under Apache 2.0, and [Python](https://www.python.org/psf/) under the PSF License, with a foundation that funds user groups: $655,000 in grants to 257 groups in 61 countries in 2024.

### Databases

[SQLite](https://sqlite.org/mostdeployed.html) estimates over one trillion SQLite databases in active use, as of April 2026: more than 4 billion smartphones, each with hundreds of them. [PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/) covers the server side. [Valkey](https://valkey.io/) shows what happens when a license changes: the Linux Foundation [launched it on March 28, 2024](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-launches-open-source-valkey-community), from Redis 7.2.4, the last version under the BSD license.

### Infrastructure

[curl](https://curl.se/) runs in over twenty billion installations, as of September 2026, by its author's count. [Kubernetes](https://kubernetes.io/) runs the containers and [nginx](https://nginx.org/) serves the pages.

### Frameworks and libraries

This is where most developers meet open source first, as a dependency: [React](https://react.dev/), [Django](https://www.djangoproject.com/), [Astro](https://astro.build/) or [Bootstrap](https://getbootstrap.com/). Bootstrap alone was downloaded 6,954,089 times from npm in the week of September 22 to 28, 2026.

### Developer tools

[Git](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Getting-Started-A-Short-History-of-Git) exists because a tool wasn't open: the Linux kernel used BitKeeper until 2005, when its free-of-charge license was revoked. [VS Code](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode) is a subtler case: its source, "Code - OSS", is MIT, and the Visual Studio Code you download is Microsoft's build of it, under a Microsoft product license. [Neovim](https://neovim.io/) is open all the way down.

### Desktop apps

[Blender](https://fund.blender.org/) is funded by its users: €281,282 a month from 7,666 individuals and 44 companies, as of September 2026. [GIMP](https://www.gimp.org/), [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) and [Krita](https://krita.org/) cover images, office documents and painting.

### Games and engines

[Godot](https://godotengine.org/) is a game engine under the MIT license, funded the same way: €35,259 a month from 1,818 members of its [Development Fund](https://fund.godotengine.org/), as of September 2026.

### Security

[OpenSSL](https://www.openssl.org/) is the TLS library behind a large part of the web's HTTPS. [Heartbleed](https://www.openssl.org/news/secadv/20140407.txt), a bug in it disclosed on April 7, 2014, showed how little funding it had: on May 29, 2014, the Linux Foundation's Core Infrastructure Initiative [announced funding](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/press-release/the-linux-foundations-core-infrastructure-initiative-announces-new-backers-first-projects-to-receive-support-and-advisory-board-members) for two full-time OpenSSL developers. [Wireshark](https://www.wireshark.org/) is on the other side: the tool you use to see what's on the wire.

## Beyond software, the source is a design, a dataset or a text

### Hardware

[Arduino](https://support.arduino.cc/hc/en-us/articles/4415094490770) publishes most of its board designs under CC BY-SA 4.0, so a board derived from one must be open too. [RISC-V](https://riscv.org/) opens one level lower, the instruction set of a processor: more than 4,500 members across 70 countries, as of October 2024.

### Data

[OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/stats/data_stats.html) is a map of the world anyone can edit: 10,895,164 registered users on September 30, 2026. [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:Statistics) is the same idea for facts: 123,517,303 items, as of September 2026.

### Content

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:Statistics) had 7,246,018 articles in English as of September 2026, each under a Creative Commons license. [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/about/) is to content what the MIT or GPL license is to code: a ready-made text that says what others can do with your work.

### Science

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/stats/monthly_submissions) has received 3,190,690 papers as of September 30, 2026, free to read before any journal publishes them. [Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/about) came out of IPython in 2014 and runs the notebooks much of that research is done in. [Zenodo](https://about.zenodo.org/), launched by CERN and OpenAIRE in May 2013, archives the data and code behind a paper, and its own code is open too.

### Education

[Moodle](https://stats.moodle.org/) is an open-source learning platform: 147,221 registered sites in 236 countries, as of September 2026. The famous course libraries are another matter: [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/about/) and [Khan Academy](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202262954) use CC BY-NC-SA. **NonCommercial means free to read and share, not open**: the [definition](/guide/what-is-open-source/definition-of-open-source/) doesn't allow restrictions on who uses a work, or for what.

### Government

Italy asks its public bodies to publish their software and describe it in a [`publiccode.yml`](https://yml.publiccode.tools/) file, which feeds the national [Developers Italia catalogue](https://catalogo-software.developers.italia.it/). The United States had code.gov; as of September 2026, the address redirects to a page about the Federal Source Code Policy.

### Standards

The web runs on open standards, written in the open. [HTML](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/) is a living standard, updated on September 29, 2026. HTTP is defined by the IETF in [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html), June 2022. [SPDX](https://spdx.dev/about/overview/), the format for listing the licenses in a project, became the international standard ISO/IEC 5962 in 2021.

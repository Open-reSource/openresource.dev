We start with your project's values, the core principles that make it tick. Then come naming, with advice on choosing a name that sticks, and logos, with the anatomy of a memorable design.

Identity goes beyond aesthetics, so we also cover consistent branding across your project. Your website plays a central role, and we show how to optimize it.

Building a community is an art in itself. We discuss a positive culture, because open source is about the people behind the code and the content. Social media is a strong outreach tool, and events and conferences add to your project's visibility.

## Defining Project Values

Start with **defining your project's values**. They lay the foundation for everything your project represents, so noble-sounding words on a page won't do.

**Clear values** act as your project's guiding light. They're the principles that steer every decision, your North Star through the open source galaxy.

Start by asking what your project stands for. Is it accessibility, transparency, collaboration, or a mix? **Pin down your core principles**, and make sure they align with the project's purpose. They set the rules of engagement for everyone involved.

Then **communicate your values clearly**. Draft them like a manifesto, as a beacon for contributors and users rather than a formality. Don't assume people will guess what you're about. Lay it out, plain and simple.

Well-defined values pay off in the real world. A contributor joins your project, drawn by what you stand for as much as by the code or content. That builds a community that's here for the shared principles as well as the tech.

Don't treat your values like stone tablets. As your project evolves, so can they. **Stay open to reassessing and refining them**. They're a living document that grows with your project.

## Crafting a Memorable Project Name

A name is a critical step in creating a memorable identity. It's the initial encounter that sets the tone for your project's presence. With millions of projects in the competitive open source world, first impressions carry significant weight.

Memorability is the key criterion. **Aim for simplicity and ease of recall**, and avoid jargon or complex acronyms. A name that rolls off the tongue contributes to your project's lasting impact.

Be clear, too. Your project's name should hint at its purpose, so users don't have to play detective. **A clear and straightforward name enhances accessibility and user understanding**.

Strive for uniqueness. **Your project's name should stand out from the crowd**. Check domain availability and existing GitHub or GitLab repositories, and run a quick Google search to make sure the name is original.

Mind the legal side. Before finalizing a name, **run a full trademark check to avoid potential legal issues** later. It saves your project trouble down the road.

Think big and future-proof the name. Your project may start small, but **choose a name that can scale as the project grows**. Avoid names that suit a small script but fall short for a bigger project.

Ask for feedback. Gather opinions from your network, including tech-savvy friends, community members, or anyone with a fresh perspective. **External input can provide valuable insights**.

Test the name before you commit. **Drop it into your developer circles, observe reactions, and be ready to make adjustments**. A flexible naming process gives a better fit.

Your project's name is an introduction, so let creativity guide you to a name that resonates and gives your project its place in the open source community.

## Designing a Distinctive Logo

Designing a logo that stands out in the crowded digital space is more art than science, but there's a blueprint.

Your logo is the visual representation of your project. **Start with a clear purpose.** What do you want it to convey? Speed, reliability, innovation? That clarity gives the logo impact.

**Simplicity is king.** A cluttered logo is like a messy codebase: no one wants to deal with it. **Keep it clean and straightforward.** Think about iconic logos like Apple or Nike, simple yet instantly recognizable.

Your logo will be everywhere, from your project's website to social media profiles and potentially swag. **Design with versatility in mind.** It should look as sharp on a small favicon as it does blown up on a conference banner.

Colors evoke emotions and perceptions, so choose wisely. **Understand color psychology**: blue for trust, green for nature, red for energy. Align your colors with your project's vibe.

Stand out, but stay relevant to your project. **Uniqueness doesn't mean randomness.** The logo should resonate with your project's identity.

Tech trends come and go, but your logo should stand the test of time. **Opt for timelessness over chasing the latest design fad.** You want it to age like fine wine, not sour milk.

Unless you're a design maestro, consider enlisting professional help. **A well-designed logo is an investment**, and a professional touch can improve your project's visual identity.

## Establishing Consistent Branding

Consistent branding makes your project visually cohesive and unforgettable. It means a unified visual experience, not your logo slapped everywhere.

Consistent branding rests on repetition. **Repetition is your best friend**, so place your visual elements where they can't be ignored. The goal is to make sure users meet your brand consistently, without bombarding them.

A style guide is your project's rulebook. **Establish a style guide** covering logo usage, color codes, and font choices. It keeps everyone on the same page and gives your project a harmonious visual identity.

Your logo is the face of your project, not a random sticker. **Place your logo** on all platforms: your website header, social media profiles, emails. Make sure it's there, loud and clear.

Colors have vibes, and you want your project to exude the right ones. **Maintain color harmony**: choose a palette and stick with it, on your website, marketing materials, or merchandise.

Fonts speak volumes about your project's personality. **Pick a font or two and stick with them**. Consistent fonts give a unified and professional look across all your project's communications.

## Crafting an Engaging Project Website

Your project website is your digital HQ, the virtual space where your project's story unfolds. A good one draws people in and keeps them hooked.

Your homepage is the VIP entrance. **Make it punchy and informative.** State what your project is about, what it solves, and why someone should care. First impressions matter, so make it count.

Keep your navigation clean and straightforward. **Smooth navigation is user-friendly.** Nobody wants to get lost in a maze of links. Highlight the essentials: about, documentation, and how to contribute.

Content is king, even on your project website. **Craft compelling content** that's easy to understand. Skip the tech jargon and speak the language of your audience. Use visuals, examples, and maybe a sprinkle of humor to keep things interesting.

Don't leave your visitors hanging. **Include clear calls-to-action (CTAs).** Invite them to contribute, download, or join the community, and guide them on the next steps. Make it easy for them to engage.

Show, don't just tell. **Incorporate visuals that tell your project's story.** Screenshots, diagrams, or an explanatory video add a dynamic touch and convey information more effectively.

Optimize for responsiveness. **Ensure your website looks good on all devices.** Whether someone opens it on a desktop, tablet, or smartphone, a smooth experience improves engagement.

## Cultivating a Community Culture

The way a project treats people is part of its identity, as much as its name or its logo. That means how fast a first pull request gets a reply, where questions go, and who gets thanked in the release notes. [Running a Community Without It Running You](/guide/maintaining/community/) covers the channels, the credit and the conflicts.

## Utilizing Social Media for Outreach

Social media outreach for your open-source project is about strategic engagement. Memes and cat videos have their place, but they're not the point.

First, **know your platforms.** Not all social media is created equal. X is great for quick updates, LinkedIn for a professional vibe, and Reddit for in-depth discussions. Tailor your content to fit the platform.

Consistency isn't only for code commits. **Post regularly and consistently.** Whether it's project updates, community highlights, or tech insights, keep the content flowing. Consistency builds momentum.

In the vast sea of tweets and posts, **visuals grab attention.** Use eye-catching graphics, screenshots, or short videos to make your content stand out. A picture is worth a thousand words, and in the fast-scrolling world of social media, visuals make a difference.

Engage with your audience. **Reply to comments, ask questions, and encourage conversations.** Social media is a two-way street. The more interactive and responsive you are, the more you'll build a community around your project.

Show the human side. **Share behind-the-scenes glimpses, celebrate milestones, and introduce the faces behind the code.** People connect with people, even online.

Use hashtags wisely. **Research and use relevant hashtags** to make your content easier to discover. Don't overdo it: keep them relevant and focused.

Last, track your performance. **Use analytics to understand what's working and what's not.** Adjust your strategy based on what you learn, and be ready to adapt.

## Participating in Events and Conferences

Events and conferences are about making meaningful connections and spreading the word, more than donning a lanyard and collecting swag (though who doesn't love swag?).

First, **select the events and conferences** you want to join with care. Don't just go for the biggest names; consider the ones most aligned with your project. Quality over quantity is the name of the game.

> Check out our [Events](/resources/events) page.

At the event, **prepare your elevator pitch**. Condense your project's essence into a brief pitch you can deliver in the time it takes to ride an elevator. Make it catchy, clear, and intriguing.

Networking is key. **Engage with fellow attendees, speakers, and sponsors.** Don't stick to your project's bubble; connect with diverse people. You never know where your next collaboration or contributor might come from.

Set up a booth if possible. **A physical presence makes your project tangible**. Use the space for demos and informative materials, and talk to visitors. Make your booth a magnet for curious minds.

Attend relevant sessions and panels. **Stay informed about industry trends and advancements.** It improves your knowledge and puts your project in the broader context of technological developments.

Use social media during events. **Live-tweet, share updates, and use event hashtags**. It amplifies your project's presence beyond the venue.

After the event, **follow up with your new connections**. Whether by email, LinkedIn, or your project's communication channels, keep the conversation going.

## Do this now

<Checklist id="building-a-strong-project-identity">

- [ ] Write your project's values in three to five lines, where contributors will read them: the README or the contributing guide.
- [ ] Check your name before you commit to it: the domain, GitHub and GitLab, a web search, a trademark search.
- [ ] Look at your logo as a favicon and in one color. If it doesn't survive both, simplify it.
- [ ] Write a one-page style guide: how to use the logo, the color codes, the fonts.
- [ ] Open your homepage as a stranger would. The first screen says what the project does, what it solves, and how to contribute.

</Checklist>

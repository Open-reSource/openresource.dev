Sixteen books on open source, in four groups, each with one line on who it is for. **Free** means the full text is online at no cost. We checked the links in October 2026.

## Start here if you want to contribute or begin a project

- [Approachable Open Source](https://approachableopensource.com/) by Brian Muenzenmeyer · 2024 · paid – **Read this if** you want to make a project welcoming to newcomers. It covers practices for maintainers and contributors.
- [Forge Your Future with Open Source](https://pragprog.com/titles/vbopens/) by VM (Vicky) Brasseur · 2018 · paid – **Read this if** you have never contributed. It walks through choosing a project, finding a task and getting a first change merged.
- [The Open Source Way](https://theopensourceway.org/) by the Open Source Way contributors · version 2.3 · free – **Read this if** you are starting or growing a community. It is written as a guidebook, and each section stands on its own.
- [Open Source Projects: Beyond Code](https://www.packtpub.com/en-us/product/open-source-projects-beyond-code-9781837636884) by John Mertic · 2023 · paid – **Read this if** your project has code and users but no governance, hosting or contributor path yet. It covers those topics for open source projects.

## Books on maintaining a project

- [Producing Open Source Software](https://producingoss.com/) by Karl Fogel · 2005 · free – **Read this if** you maintain a project. It covers running one: communication, decision-making, credit and conflict.
- [The Art of Community](https://artofcommunityonline.org/) by Jono Bacon · 2009 · first edition free (CC BY-NC-SA) – **Read this if** you are the person who has to grow and keep a community. The author, Jono Bacon, worked as a community manager and writes from that experience.
- [Uncurled](https://un.curl.dev) by Daniel Stenberg · 2022 · free – **Read this if** you want to see how curl is run. Daniel Stenberg, its lead maintainer, tells the story of the project.
- [Working in Public: The Making and Maintenance of Open-Source Software](https://press.stripe.com/working-in-public) by Nadia Eghbal · 2020 · paid – **Read this if** you want to read how the relationship between maintainers and users works on GitHub. The book covers the roles people play in a project and how code is shared.

## Money, business and law

- [Roads and Bridges: The Unseen Labor Behind Our Digital Infrastructure](https://www.fordfoundation.org/learning/library/research-reports/roads-and-bridges-the-unseen-labor-behind-our-digital-infrastructure/) by Nadia Eghbal · 2016 · free report – **Read this if** you need a source for the funding of open source infrastructure. The Ford Foundation published the report, and it looks at who maintains the code and how they are paid.
- [How Open Source Ate Software](https://link.springer.com/978-1-4842-6799-8) by Gordon Haff · 2018, second edition 2021 · paid – **Read this if** you work at a company that uses open source. It covers business models and governance.
- [Open Source Law, Policy and Practice](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/59819) edited by Amanda Brock · second edition 2022 · free (open access) – **Read this if** you handle licensing, trademarks or patents. It is a collection of chapters by legal practitioners, published open access.

## History and ideas

- [The Cathedral and the Bazaar](http://www.catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/) by Eric S. Raymond · 1999 · free essay – **Read this if** you want the essay on development in the open that Eric S. Raymond published in 1999. It compares two ways of building software: the cathedral and the bazaar.
- [Free as in Freedom](https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/5768) by Sam Williams · 2002 · free (GNU Free Documentation License) – **Read this if** you want the biography of Richard Stallman. It tells how the free software movement started.
- [Rebel Code](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/glyn-moody/rebel-code/9780738206707/) by Glyn Moody · 2001 · paid – **Read this if** you want the history of Linux and the open source movement. It draws on interviews and stops in 2001.
- [Free Culture](http://free-culture.cc/) by Lawrence Lessig · 2004 · free – **Read this if** you want the copyright background of open licenses. Lawrence Lessig writes about copyright and culture beyond software.
- [Coders](https://www.clivethompson.net/) by Clive Thompson · 2019 · paid – **Read this if** you want a book on programmer culture. Open source is one of the topics it covers.
